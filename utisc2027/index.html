The Competition

What is UTISC?

UTISC is the first and only national collegiate sales competition built exclusively for non-seniors. Freshmen, sophomores, and juniors compete in live, 15-minute sales role plays with a buyer while judges score them. It is certified by the University Sales Center Alliance (USCA), and a record 144 students competed in 2026.

Two divisions

Capital sends one competitor to each division: Sophomore/Freshman and Junior. UTISC sets your division by the higher-education credit hours you have going in, not your informal class standing. Seniors do not compete in role plays but can serve as peer coaches.

How you're scored

Half your score comes from understanding the customer and presenting the right solution. This is consultative selling: listening, asking smart questions, and solving a business problem. It is not a memorized pitch.

Opening & rapport5%Professional intro, rapport, an agenda with a business reason

Selling International Paper

The value isn't the box. It's what the box helps the customer accomplish.

International Paper (IP) is the UTISC product sponsor. UTISC releases the 2027 prospect profile to universities in late November or early December, and the internal competition on January 8 will use it. Start now with the 2025 case: the product, the customers, and the selling skills carry over.

About International Paper

  • Transforms renewable resources into products people use every day, from packaging to pulp for absorbent products
  • World-leading producer of fiber-based products, with 125 years of manufacturing expertise
  • Global leader in corrugated packaging that is reliable, renewable, and customizable
  • Packaging that protects and promotes goods, enables commerce, helps keep consumers safe, and supports recycling

Who buys corrugated packaging?

Any business that uses corrugated boxes, displays, or containers: manufacturers, fulfillment centers, distributors, and retailers across every industry.

Common uses

  • Shipping boxes
  • Presentation and display boxes for kits, subscription services, gifts, and retail shelf displays
  • Product containers for a single product or a set of products

Four solutions, four business challenges

Custom corrugated packaging

Challenge: protect the product and fit the customer's operation.

  • Options for size, shape, fluting, slotting, assembly, and die cutting
  • Rigorously tested
  • Design services: graphic, structural, mechanical packaging, fulfillment (co-packing), and industry-specific packaging and displays
On-box graphics & engagement

Challenge: stand out and engage customers.

  • High graphic capability on the inside and outside of the package
  • Serialized QR codes and bar codes printed directly on the box
  • Can eliminate a labeling step and provide tailored customer engagement
eBOS: e-Commerce Box Optimization Service

Challenge: ship a variable product mix efficiently.

  • Analyzes shipment history and box utilization
  • Recommends a suite of right-sized boxes
  • Reduces freight costs, dunnage, product damage, and carbon footprint; improves warehouse efficiency and the unboxing experience
Sustainability

Challenge: meet environmental objectives.

  • Recyclable and made from renewable resources
  • Circular solutions that enable recycling and reduce waste
  • Right-sizing helps reduce carbon footprint

How eBOS works

  1. Collect the customer's shipment history
  2. Run the eBOS algorithm
  3. IP designers review and approve
  4. Recommend a new, right-sized box suite

Images and process: International Paper eBOS sales sheet.

Let SPIN questions choose the solution

IP offers several possible solutions, and you won't have time to pitch them all. Your Situation, Problem, Implication, and Need-payoff questions should tell you which avenue matters to this buyer. Listen for the signals, then go deep on one or two.

The box itself

Listen for: damage, returns, durability, hard-to-assemble boxes, labor and training.

  • S: "What do orders ship in today?"
  • P: "How often do products arrive damaged?"
  • I: "What does a damaged order cost you in replacements and reviews?"
  • N: "What would cutting damage in half mean for your team?"

Solution: custom corrugated packaging: size, shape, fluting, assembly, die cutting, rigorous testing.

Printing & design

Listen for: brand image, unboxing experience, plain or outdated packaging.

  • S: "How is your brand shown on the box today?"
  • P: "Does your current packaging match your brand?"
  • I: "What happens when the first impression feels generic?"
  • N: "How would a branded unboxing help retention?"

Solution: single or multicolor printing, graphic design, and structural design services.

On-box advertising

Listen for: customer engagement, repeat purchases, promotions, app downloads.

  • S: "How do you reach customers after delivery?"
  • P: "What's hard about getting repeat orders?"
  • I: "What does a missed reorder cost over a year?"
  • N: "What if the box itself drove people back to your app?"

Solution: high-graphic printing inside and out, plus serialized QR codes and bar codes printed on the box.

Sustainability

Listen for: Gen Z values, recycling, waste, corporate goals, plastic.

  • S: "What sustainability goals is your team measured on?"
  • P: "Where does packaging fall short of those goals?"
  • I: "How do customers react to excess packaging or plastic?"
  • N: "What would fully recyclable packaging mean for your brand?"

Solution: recyclable packaging from renewable resources, curbside accepted, circular solutions.

eBOS right-sizing

Listen for: shipping costs, oversized boxes, void fill, many box sizes, warehouse speed.

  • S: "How many box sizes do your packers choose from?"
  • P: "How much filler goes into a typical order?"
  • I: "If freight keeps rising, what happens to your margins?"
  • N: "What would right-sized boxes save across 23 fulfillment centers?"

Solution: eBOS analyzes shipment history and recommends a right-sized box suite.

Fulfillment services

Listen for: new programs like subscriptions, kitting, limited staff, speed to launch.

  • S: "Who will pack the subscription boxes?"
  • P: "Do you have the staff and space for a new program?"
  • I: "What happens if launch slips because packing isn't ready?"
  • N: "How would outsourced packing help you launch faster?"

Solution: fulfillment services (co-packing): quick pick & pack or thoughtful pack.

Sample questions are practice starters. Solutions come from the 2025 prospect profile and the eBOS sales sheet.

Same company. Same products. Different buyers. Should the sales conversation be the same? Each meeting is 15 minutes, so plan your questions around what this buyer cares about.

How to sell a box: discover, connect, advance

1Discover

Ask questions that uncover the business problem.

  • "How are you measuring packaging cost today?"
  • "How much filler goes into a typical order?"
  • "How often do products arrive damaged, and what does that cost?"
  • "What sustainability goals is your team measured on?"
  • "If this continues, what does it mean for next year?"
→
2Connect

Tie what they said to the right solution, in their words: "You mentioned rising freight costs and a lot of filler, so let me show you how eBOS right-sizes your boxes."

→
3Advance

Summarize their priorities and ask for a logical next step, like sharing shipment history for an eBOS review or meeting with other decision makers.

Build our team objection bank

Practice answering these with clarify, address, confirm. Bring new ones to practice and post them in GroupMe.

  • "We already have a packaging supplier."
  • "That costs more than our plain kraft boxes."
  • "Changing boxes would disrupt our warehouse."
  • "I'm not the person who decides this."
  • "We're not sure the subscription program will launch."
  • "Send me some information and I'll look at it."

FAQ

Questions, answered

Do I need sales or competition experience?

No. No experience necessary. What matters is curiosity, commitment, and a willingness to improve. Practice and coaching do the rest.

Can seniors participate?

Seniors can't compete in UTISC role plays, but they can serve as peer coaches and travel with the team.

Which division am I in?

UTISC sets your division by the higher-education credit hours you have going into the competition, not your class label. Ask Professor Jacobs if you're unsure.

How are competitors and alternates chosen?

At the internal competition on Friday, January 8, 11:00 AM–2:00 PM. The top performer in each division becomes the competitor. Alternates are chosen by lottery.

What does an alternate do?

Alternates prepare exactly like competitors, travel with the team, and must be ready to compete if needed. They also take part in the career fair, development sessions, and team activities.

Why are we preparing with the 2025 case if the internal competition uses the 2027 case?

International Paper is the sponsor again, so the products, customers, and selling skills carry over. UTISC releases the 2027 prospect profile in late November or early December. Learning IP with the 2025 case now means you can focus on the new scenario as soon as it arrives.

What if I'm chosen as an alternate or peer coach and don't want to go?

By signing the trip agreement, you commit to the trip regardless of your role, and payment is non-refundable.

What if I can't pay by January 8?

Students who haven't paid by January 8 won't be permitted to go on the trip. If it's a hardship, talk with Professor Jacobs privately before the due date.

What does the $275 cover?

The competition entry fee and your share of a hotel room. UTISC provides Thursday evening food, Friday lunch, Friday dinner, and Saturday lunch. Transportation, other meals, and personal spending are on you.

How will we get to Toledo?

In past years, we've caravanned to Toledo in our own vehicles. Professor Jacobs can drive three students in her CR-V. Beyond that, we'll work together to set up carpools with students who are willing to drive. If you can drive, let Professor Jacobs know or post in GroupMe. Transportation isn't included in the trip fee.

Who will I room with?

Rooms are shared by two students. Room assignments will be shared before the trip.

What should I wear?

Business professional on Thursday evening, all day Friday, and Saturday. Business casual for the Friday night entertainment and dinner. Casual clothes are fine for traveling.

Do I need a resume?

Yes. UTISC collects resumes by January 29 and shares them with sponsors in February, so have yours polished before then. Schedule a review with Capital Career Development, and bring printed copies to the career fair.

Can I network if I'm not competing?

Yes. Everyone attends the career fair and events with sponsor companies. Capital students have landed internships and full-time jobs through UTISC connections, including students who didn't compete.

What if I miss a practice?

Let Professor Jacobs know early and catch up through GroupMe. Consistent practice is how we win as a team.
